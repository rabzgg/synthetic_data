# Pre-sales: picture a client's dataset before the real CSV exists

A prospective client says "we measure A and B in situation C, with sensor X", but has not sent a CSV
yet. The app builds a believable synthetic version of their data: the columns they log, in their
situation, from the sensors they name or, until they do, from a typical sensor of each class. The
information arrives in this order, and the app follows it:

| what we know | when | in the app |
|---|---|---|
| what is measured and the situation | always, first | **Start from columns**: pick the columns, then a situation (a world that provides all of them) |
| the sensor's name | sometimes | per column, a named part instead of **Generic** |
| the datasheet | later | a part file from it (`docs/HOW_TO_ADD_A_SENSOR.md`, `docs/prompts/DATASHEET_TO_PART.md`); it then shows up per column |
| the real CSV | last | calibration (see **Next**, not done yet) |

## The flow in the UI

1. **Columns.** Grouped as Air (climate and gas), IMU (motion), Electrical, Light, Machine, Soil, Water,
   each with its column and unit.
2. **Environment.** Only the worlds that provide every chosen column (`configs/worlds/`).
3. **Sensor per column.** "Generic (<class>)" or a part from a datasheet that measures it.
   One part chosen for several columns is one part (a BME280 for temperature and humidity). What it
   also measures but nobody chose is not written and not simulated ("also measures X, not written").
4. **Sampling.** The logging interval, and per column resolution, RMS noise and ODR, shown and editable.
   A change is written as "ASSUMPTION: user tweak" (on screen: "saved as assumed"). A box writes the
   readings as **counts (LSB)**: round(reading / resolution) as an integer, signed where the range goes
   below zero. That is the compensated reading in LSB, not the raw ADC code of a sensor that needs
   compensation formulas (the BME280's raw ADC values are uncompensated 20-bit codes).
5. **Name, events, generate.** Events the device can show in that environment, then plots, the checks,
   the event labels, the **data summary**, a ZIP, and "Save this device" (a file in `configs/devices/`).

The same in Python:

```python
from core.devices import device_from_columns
dev = device_from_columns({"temperature_c": "bosch_bme280", "co2_ppm": "generic"}, name="Office sensor",
                          sample_interval_s=60, raw_counts=False)
```

## Noise follows the sample rate

A sensor whose output data rate (ODR) can be set runs at the device's sample rate, as a real device
logging at 25 Hz would configure it: the ODR is 1 / sample interval (a generic class: clamped to its
`odr_range_hz`; a datasheet part: the first of its `odr_choices_hz` or `odr_settings_hz` at or above
the rate), the bandwidth is ODR / 2 (or the datasheet's filter for that ODR, the wider one where an
ODR has two: BMG160 register 0x10), and a noise given as a density (`noise_density`) gives noise sd = density ×
√bandwidth. A generic accelerometer has 4 mg RMS at 100 Hz and 2 mg at 25 Hz. Not when:

- the device pins `odr_hz` (or `bandwidth_hz`) in its overrides, or the user changes the ODR in the UI;
- the part has a fixed rate (gas cells, NDIR CO2, transmitters, probes: no `odr_choices_hz` /
  `odr_range_hz`) or a fixed filter bandwidth from its datasheet;
- the quantity is not logged but only read by logic (the G7c's accelerometer for fall detection runs
  at its own 2000 Hz).

The manifest records the ODR and bandwidth in use and where they come from
(`parts[].quantities.<q>.rate`). A sensor that follows the sample rate makes a new value every
1 / ODR, also faster than the world's grid (0.04 s for motion, 1 s for air; the truth is
interpolated). A fixed-rate sensor logged faster than it updates repeats its last value, as a real
logger reading it would.

## Data summary (`_summary.json`, and in the UI)

Per column of every run (`core/output/summary.py`), read from the CSV and the manifest:

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `rows`, `sample_rate_hz` | rows in the file, 1 / sample interval |
| `unit`, `min`, `max` | as written (`counts` for raw counts) |
| `bits` | readings: ceil(log2(span / resolution + 1)) from the sensor's range and resolution |
| `noise_over_resolution` | the part's noise sd at the ODR in use / its resolution |
| `odr_hz`, `bandwidth_hz` | the rate and bandwidth in use (sources in the manifest) |
| `generic` | the column comes from a generic part |

## What to tell a client (and what not)

- The data are synthetic: `realism: ASSUMPTION` on every world. They follow the datasheets (or, for
  generic parts, the stated class assumptions) and the world's rules; they are not fitted to field
  recordings.
- The sample interval, the resolution and the noise shape the data most. Those are the first
  questions to ask a client.

## Next: calibrate with the real CSV (not done yet)

When the client's CSV arrives:

1. Compare the characteristics of the data per column, theirs against ours: noise (sd of the
   differences on quiet stretches), step size (the smallest change seen, i.e. the real resolution),
   range (min and max), and autocorrelation (how slowly the signal moves).
2. Adjust the part (resolution, noise, t90, ODR) and the world (levels, daily cycle, events) until
   they match within a stated tolerance; record each change with its source ("fitted to client CSV
   <name>").
3. Report the characteristics of both side by side.

None of this is implemented yet; it needs a calibration script and an agreed tolerance per measure.
